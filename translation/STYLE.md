# Style guide for Owen Theologoumena Pantodapa translation segments

Audience: a Reformed Baptist pastor and PhD student. Register: formal, precise, readable scholarly English, mildly modernized (use "you," not "thee/thou").

FORMAT (Markdown):
- Book headings in the Latin ("Liber IV" etc. with their Latin subtitle) -> "# BOOK IV. " followed by the subtitle translated.
- Chapter headings: "Caput I" plus its argument (the long summary with dashes) -> "# Chapter I" on one line, then the argument translated as a single paragraph beginning "Chapter argument: ".
- Page markers: the Latin embeds printed page numbers as "  p 156  ". Where one falls between sections, put "## [ p. 156 ]" on its own line. Where it falls mid-paragraph, insert "[ p. 156 ]" inline at that point.
- Keep Owen's Roman numeral section numbers at paragraph start: "I. ", "II. ", etc.
- Verse quotations (Latin or Greek poetry): translate as quoted prose in "double quotes" with the citation in parentheses, e.g. (Horace, Odes 3.30.7).

LANGUAGES:
- Translate ALL Greek and Hebrew. Where the argument turns on the original wording, add a bracket: [Greek: transliteration] or [Hebrew: transliteration]. Do not reproduce long Greek passages untranslated.
- Owen often quotes a Greek author then gives his own Latin rendering: translate once, smoothly, noting "He says" etc.
- Scripture references in standard form: (Gen. 3:15), (Heb. 2:14).

OTHER RULES:
- Bracketed clarifications [like this] sparingly, only where the reader needs them.
- Translator's notes in brackets for obscure allusions, e.g. [Aurum Tolosanum: the plundered sacred gold proverbial for bringing ruin].
- Do NOT use em dashes in your own prose; use commas, colons, semicolons, or parentheses instead.
- NEVER summarize, condense, or skip. This is a complete translation. Every sentence of the Latin must be represented.
